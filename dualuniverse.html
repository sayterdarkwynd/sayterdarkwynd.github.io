Design for Dual Universe

A spacefaring MMO with voxel-based crafting and deep physics, every design on this project was manually built, block by block through its voxel crafting system. My primary responsibilities were crafting the FTUE (First Time User Experience) tutorial areas, building player hubs and instances, and building spacecraft for use by players as templates within the in-game markets.

I was in charge of the design and scripting for the Mining, Building and Market tutorials - no small task, as these systems were all complicated. Of the three, the voxel building tutorials were the most complex - there were many tricks and techniques a player would need to learn to make the most of the system. I devised a 'clean-room' style approach that I would use for both the Basic and Advanced versions. Each room would teach a separate technique of the Build Tool, and challenge the player to use that knowledge before progressing. To reduce cognitive load, I split this into two separate tutorials (Basic and Advanced, respectively.)

The Basic Building rooms taught the basics of choosing a block in the player inventory, and placing it in a space to form a specific voxel shape. Players advanced through these in sequence.

The second room taught players how to cut or copy shapes from existing voxel builds, and paste them in other locations.

The first room to pull on what they learned in previous sections, I tasked players with using the copy/paste technique, paired with voxel placement, to create combinations of polygon shapes they copied from the corners. Once completed, the exit opened.

I iterated on this learning in the following room - players were presented with miscellaneous neon-green shapes, and had to mimic each one in sequence. The last would teach them how to deform voxels to create an angular effect simply through placement.

The final room changed things up. Here, they were given a selection of placeable objects - a door and some windows. They were instructed on how to use them. Once placed in accordance with the tutorial requirements, the door opened.

The player has access outside the building now, and had to locate the exit - this is where we instructed them on how to locate and follow waypoints.

In the Advanced tutorial, the player expanded on existing Build Tool knowledge, and learned how to create complex shapes, smooth voxels, add or remove volume, change selected tiles, and more.

The Advanced Build Tutorial allowed players chose the order of completion, and tackle more advanced elements of voxelcraft.

The Smoothing Tool room taught the basics of the smoothe tool, and tasks the player with copying this cat shape, and then smoothing it out.

In this room, players were taught how to target an area and apply a smoothing filter to the entire area rather than small, targetted segments.

I then tasked the player with learning the advanced aspects of the copy/paste tool.

Finally, by using voxels to connect colored zones of matching block colors, I taught players to create angular components, and complex polygons.

A unique challenge of working Dual Universe that truly engaged the entire level design team was a Megastructure we simply called 'the Cube'. This kilometer-wide dungeon was a massive maze containing many complex puzzles using the games wiring. I began this herculean task by constructing the initial entrance section and exterior greebles for the enormous object.

The final entrance to scale, and thousands of greebled details.

The first puzzle - hidden switches that had to be triggered in a sequence to open the forcefield doors into the primary entrance shaft, was within the lobby area. I also used CSS, HTML and digital art to create custom screen interfaces to convey data to the player.
