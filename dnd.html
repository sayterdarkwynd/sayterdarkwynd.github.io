The Vault of Ages

I've been drawing and designing magic items for the new RPG Daggerheart from Darrington Press. Design for this tabletop game requires clear and concise rules for mechanics that are player facing - The game is designed with the intention of using cards for player abilities and features, so space is limited to a 2.5 x 3.5 inch space.

Vault of Ages PDF Heart of Daggers Page

Since I was creating magic items more powerful than those already provided in Daggerheart, I made several adjustments for balance, while keeping as close as possible to the existing card designs. Using the existing game mechanics to avoid needless complexity, I made the following design choices to implement these creations:

  • • Players are limited to 5 active class feature cards at any one time. These items replace these cards, encouraging strategic choice and player agency and avoiding power creep.
  • • Class features have a 'Recall' cost paid by the games 'Stress' resource in order for players to add it into their 'Vault'. I carried this mechanic over onto these items.
  • • Apart from a few specific items, the vast majority had to be viable for at least four character classes and encourage interesting synergies.
  • • As items in this game are normally just written on a character sheet, I had to provide space for damage, armor or other stats where necessary.

Map Design

Through digital art, illustration, Dungeondraft, or other means I have created hundreds of maps, scenarios and encounters that provide engrossing moments for players and gamemasters alike.

An isometric one-page dungeon, providing gamemasters with all they need to run a session without heaps of prep work. They can select a few monsters, items, and hit the ground running. It provides 4+ hours of gameplay.

All necessary guidance is provided on this sheet, with indicators in place for areas of interest.

A top down design with the same constraints as the Dungeon of the Mad Mage, with a frostier feel, and a tiny bit of interesting lore for tables to play around with in their narrative. As with the previous entry, this provides at least 4+ hours of gameplay!

Within dark, claustrophobic tunnels, players had to avoid pursuit by drakes in a minecart sequence, survive an assault by earth elementals, and enter the bottom of the mine where a murderous gray dragon awaited, corrupted by toxic fungal growths. Verticality gave players a chance for tactical superiority, while multiple chokepoints provided opportunities to surprise them with hazards.

The first floor began a minecart sequence, which continued into this second portion. Players then had to abandon the cart and wind their way through hostile earth elementals and environmental threats.

Next, they had to find a way over the large, deadly fall into darkness and wander into the lair of a corrupted grey dragon and engage in epic battle!
